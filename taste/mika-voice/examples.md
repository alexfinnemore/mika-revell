# Examples

These are calibration examples. The good ones are Mika's own sentences. The bad ones are real sentences from the site as it stood on 2026-09-29, each with a better version. Each example tests a different belief in `philosophy.md`.

## Good

1. **Opening on the object** (Softcore War, her text): "A large, inert rock sits beneath a giant, pink balloon. It sees itself reflected in the polished surface, staring back at a rose-colored world."
   - This works because the image carries the idea before any theory arrives, and the pink does something: it reflects a rose-colored world back at the rock.
2. **Ending on the reader's question** (Softcore War, her text): "What alternative worlds could we create? What is holding us back?"
   - This hands the problem back instead of issuing a moral.
3. **Paired oppositions** (Cruise Control, her text): "Did we concede to automation over autonomy, compliance over choice, convenience over freedom?"
   - Three exact pairs name the contradiction and leave it standing.
4. **A first-person account** (The Bondage of Costume, her text): "I started this work while living in Tokyo... The single word I felt best described the culture of Japan was 'contradiction'."
   - The entry is plain and personal, it turns on one precise word, and the text stays in "I" throughout.
5. **Compression** (Unnatural Disasters, her text): "From Hiroshima to the oil fields of Kuwait, each painting is a document of man's folly and Nature's fury."
   - It names the real range and holds the contradiction in one balanced sentence.

## Bad, with fixes

1. **Artspeak inflation** (Power Play, as of 2026-09-29): "Revell's latest series of artwork delves into the intriguing relationship between symbols of power and commodified objects of desire."
   - This fails on "delves" and "intriguing", and on "latest", which went stale.
   - Better: "Power Play sets symbols of power against commodified objects of desire."
2. **Mixed person** (Softcore War, as of 2026-09-29): "...Revell subverts symbols of violence with pacifism and levity. Mika employs satire and absurdity..."
   - "Revell" and "Mika" appear in consecutive sentences.
   - Better: "Revell employs satire and absurdity..."
3. **An em dash as a beat, and rating the idea** (Love Threats, as of 2026-09-29): "Revell proposes an important paradox (em dash) a war of love."
   - The dash stands in for a sentence, and "important" tells the reader how to judge.
   - Better: "Love Threats proposes a paradox: a war of love."
4. **Praising instead of describing** (Power Play, as of 2026-09-29): "Her artistry fuses seemingly oppositional concepts."
   - "Her artistry" is press-release praise.
   - Better: "The work fuses things that seem opposed."
5. **A fragment and a repeated word** (Cruise Control, as of 2026-09-29): "...a rapid, wall-sized, scrolling film of social media content and rapid audio. Creating a disorienting and unpleasant environment within the gallery."
   - "Rapid" appears twice, and the last line is a fragment.
   - Better: "...a wall-sized audio-visual work that forced the viewer into a fast, scrolling film of social media content and sound, creating a disorienting and unpleasant environment within the gallery."
6. **A slogan coined by an editor** (a draft bio line, 2026-09-29): "using pink as a panacea and absurdity as a weapon."
   - "Absurdity as a weapon" is a new aphorism made for rhythm, and Mika never wrote it.
   - Better, using her own phrases: "Using her signature pink as a panacea, she employs satire and absurdity to expose the ways we package and accept a brutal and competitive world."
