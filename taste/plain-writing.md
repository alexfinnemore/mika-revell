# Plain writing

Source: Alex's writing guides in the goose-group Smaak package (How We Write, Sentence Craft, Writing Style, Language Guide), copied here on 2026-09-29 because this repo can't reach Smaak. Only the rules that matter for this site are kept.

## Who follows what

- **Issues, READMEs, docs, commit messages and anything else written about this repo** follow every rule in this file.
- **Mika's art texts** (series statements, bio, writing pages) follow the subset in "Mika's art texts" below, because some of the rules would flatten her voice.

## The rules

1. **Write complete sentences, and let conjunctions do the work.** A finished thought has a subject and a verb, and often a "because", "while", "although" or "rather than" holding its two halves together. Chains of fragments feel energetic, but they drop the link between ideas and read as machine-written.
   - Bad: "The build is slow. Not the code. The install."
   - Good: "The build is slow because the install takes two minutes, not because of the code."
2. **No em dashes.** Where a dash is standing in for a pause, replace it with a comma, a colon, or a full stop and a new sentence.
3. **No arrows standing in for a narrative.** Write the sequence as sentences, because arrows hide the causal claim.
4. **Keep bold labels rare.** One or two in a reference document are fine, and the fixed section headings of an issue are fine. A page where every paragraph opens with a label is a form, not writing.
5. **Use a bullet list only for content that is genuinely a list,** such as dates, names, files or parallel facts. If the items have an order, a cause or a weighting, write them as a paragraph.
6. **Use nouns, not intensifiers.** "Three pages load the full-size image" can be checked. "Really slow pages" can't.
7. **No slogans, aphorisms or clever reframes.** No "it's not X, it's Y", and no "not a moonshot". Say the plain thing.
8. **No buzzwords.** Never write: unlock, leverage (as a verb), synergy, streamline, robust, seamless, holistic, best practices, scalable, paradigm, democratize, game-changer.
9. **No throat-clearing.** Cut "it's important to note that", "in order to" (write "to") and "at the end of the day".
10. **Be candid and don't invent.** Say what isn't done and what isn't known. Never fabricate a number, date or quote. Label anything unconfirmed as unconfirmed in the same sentence.
11. **Make headings state the finding.** "Hidden series pages still build" is a finding. "Hidden series" is only a topic.
12. **Lead with the point.** For issues and docs, the first sentence states the conclusion or the change.
13. **Use contractions and active voice** in issues and docs. Write the way one person talks to another.
14. **Self-check.** Count the em dashes, arrows, bold labels and verbless sentences. If they outnumber the paragraphs, the formatting is doing the writing. Then read it aloud.

## Mika's art texts

**These rules apply** to her writing: 1 (complete sentences; her closing questions are fine), 2 (no em dashes), 3, 4, 6, 7, 8 and 10.

**These don't apply,** because they would flatten her voice:
- **12, lead with the point.** She leads with an object or a question.
- **"End with a next step".** She ends on a question or an image.
- **13, contractions.** Her statements are formal and don't use contractions.
- **"Write about the subject, not to the reader".** Her rhetorical questions address the reader on purpose.
- **11, headings.** Her texts have no headings.

Her own rules sit in `taste/mika-voice/`. Where they conflict with this file, hers win for her texts.
